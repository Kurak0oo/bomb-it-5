# Coherent style, not the model, wins jams

No AI art model wins more often than its share of entries predicts, in jams or in commercial games, once sample sizes, self-reporting and sponsors are accounted for. Where it is visible, usage clusters on a few tools. OpenAI's GPT Image line is the most-named 2D model among AI-jam entries: 20 of 97 hand-coded Slapjam entries ship it in-game. Tripo and Meshy lead 3D, PixelLab leads pixel sprites, and Suno and ElevenLabs lead audio. GPT Image also tops the blind text-to-image arenas, and Suno v6 tops the blind music arenas, so in those two categories popularity and measured quality agree. Neither 2D image models nor 3D generators are over-represented among winners, though. In the pooled Slapjam top 10, GPT Image ships in 4 of 21 entries against 16 of 76 below (p = 1.0). Tripo's large podium lift in Vibe Jam 2026 rests on one game, the Gold winner, whose maker the sponsor had paid in credits to tag it. Two signals do survive at the top. Agent-drawn or procedural art is the main art source in 48% of Slapjam top-10 entries against 21% below (p ≈ 0.02). AI audio appears in 3 of Vibe Jam 2026's top 10 against 11 of 945 entries overall, the only result that comes close to surviving a multiple-comparison correction. Both signals more likely mark thorough, experienced developers who hold one coherent look and finish their sound than any model's edge. On Steam, the evidence points the other way from "a model wins": disclosing AI use at all goes with fewer and worse reviews, and fewer than one disclosure in ten names a tool. For bomb-it-5 the practical answer is clear. The replica needs no generator at all. The Douyin build should pick one image model and one style, or have the agent draw vector art in code, keep the owner as the art gate, and treat model choice as a secondary decision.

## A handful of tools covers almost every named use

The first obstacle is that almost nobody says which art model they used. **Vibe Jam 2026's `made_with` field was filled for 917 of 945 games, but entrants used it to name their coding harness.** Claude Code appears 351 times, Cursor 204 and Codex 171. Only 28 entries (2.96%) name any AI asset tool, and in 2025, when the field was empty for every row, only 5 of 1,083 do, found in free-text pitches ([vibejam.com](https://vibejam.com/); [vibejam.com/api](https://vibejam.com/api)). These are floor values. The 2025 Gold winner wrote that he used GPT-4o images for billboards, Suno for music and ElevenLabs for sound effects, and none of that appears in the jam's data ([Cassovia Code](https://cassoviacode.com/i-tried-vibe-coding-how-i-created-gta-with-artificial-intelligence/)). Steam is no better. A random sample of 1,592 unreleased store pages found that **only about 4–5% of AI disclosures name a product** ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-ai-disclosure-what-developers-wrote)). In our own count of the 1,500 most recent Steam releases (15–24 November 2026), 82 of the 834 disclosing games (9.8%) named one ([Steam search](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?sort_by=Released_DESC&category1=998)).

Three things stand out. First, **Midjourney leads only among studio professionals.** It is the top image tool in GDC's January 2026 survey, yet it appears in zero of 2,125 jam entries and in 2 Steam releases in our sample. GDC's November 2026 edition reports only "image generation 22%", "concept art 22%" and "final in-game assets 8%" with no model names ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-52b)). The gap reads as a split between concept-art users in studios and asset-shipping solo developers. It may also reflect a shift toward GPT Image and Nano Banana over 2026, but because the two sources survey different populations, that remains unproven. Second, **in AI jams GPT Image dominance follows the subscription.** Most GPT Image users in the Slapjam sample also code with Codex or ChatGPT, so the image tool comes free with the coding tool. Third, **3D generators together outnumber any single 2D model on Steam** (12 games against 8 for Gemini), and among 3D tools, which one leads depends on who sponsored what (see below). Stable Diffusion, Flux, Leonardo, Scenario, Recraft, Retro Diffusion and Udio are close to absent everywhere, even though Slapjam's own guide recommends several of them ([Slapjam guide](https://slapjam.ai/docs/)).

Outside the West, category shares are all that exist. In China, art and design tools account for **57.4% of AI-tool traffic in the games industry** ([PEdaily on Gamma Data](https://news.pedaily.cn/20260730/135291.shtml)). Japan's CESA finds 85.8% of developers using generative AI at work but gives no model breakdown ([CESA preview](https://www.cesa.or.jp/uploads/cesa_report_preview_0914.pdf)). Neither names a model.

## Winners over-index on finished audio and code-drawn art, not on any image model

Only two data sets allow a winner-versus-field comparison with per-entry tool data: Vibe Jam 2026, which has a `final_rank` for 943 games and 14 prize winners, and Slapjam #1 and #2, where I hand-coded 97 entries. Their results point the same way.

Behind these lifts are just three games. The Gold winner, *A Game About Capybaras Delivering Food*, lists "Claude Code, Tripo3d, Suno, ElevenLabs". HALDANE-4 cites an AI voice, and Kanso cites Suno. About 210 category-by-group tests were run, so the Bonferroni threshold is p < 0.00024. **Only the "any AI audio, top 10" result comes near it, and it is borderline.** Tripo's podium lift is one developer. Outside the Gold game, Tripo users sit at the base rate (top 100: 2 of 99 against 10 of 945).

The winners' own pages show what "procedural" means in practice. Slapjam #2 winner Speed Slash says "the code, the music engine and the art were all built together with Claude" ([page](https://megumih.itch.io/speed-slash)). Runner-up Escape Velocity says "All art is drawn in code at runtime (no image files)" ([page](https://behlulakdag.itch.io/escape-velocity)). ### Five confounders explain more than the models do

The patterns above can be partly explained without any model being better. **Self-reporting bias** comes first. Entrants who list a full asset pipeline are probably the thorough ones. Winners' known tool use is missing from the data, as the 2025 and 2026 Gold winners show, so lift is unreliable in both directions. **Sponsors** distort the counts. Tripo offered 3,000 credits to Vibe Jam entries that tagged it ([X @tripoai](https://x.com/tripoai/status/2043713320050655555)), and the gallery has a "MADE WITH Tripo" filter button. That alone can explain Tripo's 10-to-1 lead over Meshy there, while Meshy leads Tripo on Steam (7 to 4). Slapjam's sponsored art tools, AutoSprite and PixelLab, each appear once in 97 entries and in no placed entry. The Tripo Portal Pass jam simply required Tripo ([Slapjam rules survey](https://slapjam.ai/news/is-ai-allowed-in-game-jams/)). **Engine** is confounded with tool. Three.js made all three Vibe Jam podium games and 9 of the top 10 (65% of the field overall), and 10 of the 12 named 3D-generator users also built in Three.js ([vibejam.com](https://vibejam.com/)). **Developer experience** dominates. Earlier write-up research found the Vibe Jam winners were seasoned developers putting in weeks of polish, in a field where most entrants "never made a game before" ([levels.io](https://levels.io/winners-of-the-2025-vibe-code-game-jam)). **Format** matters too. Slapjam requires mobile-portrait HTML5, which rewards small builds with a single look, and code-drawn art provides exactly that. Organisers make the same point in their guidance: "Pick one image model and one style prompt and reuse it for everything" ([Slapjam guide](https://slapjam.ai/docs/)). Other jams score raw, unedited AI art and clashing styles low (per a search summary of [ctrl ai jam](https://itch.io/jam/ctrl-ai-jam); unverified).

The jams that could settle the question have no usable data yet. The 2026 Epic AI MegaJam drew 2,782 submissions, but its results are due by 29 January 2027, and the current popularity leader states "no AI was used to make this game" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/ai-free-fih-aura-farming-game-tops-popularity-ranking-in-epic-games-major-ai-game-jam/)). The Unity AI Game Jam in Busan was sponsored by Meshy and OpenAI, yet its winners named no art tools ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28843/three-winners-selected-for-unity-ai-game-jam-joined-by-unity-openai-and-meshy)). In Ziva's Micro Jam bracket, entries using the agent averaged 2.50 against 2.56 for the rest of the field, and no art models were named ([Ziva blog, vendor](https://ziva.sh/blogs/godot-game-jam-winners)). Micro Jam has since banned generative AI for art and audio ([Slapjam survey](https://slapjam.ai/news/ai-game-jams-2026/)).

## Blind arenas agree with usage on GPT Image and Suno, not on Midjourney or 3D

Blind pairwise arenas give a quality ranking that is independent of usage, and they line up with jam usage more closely than expected. **OpenAI's GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst leads Artificial Analysis's text-to-image arena at 1263 ±14 Elo**, and the GPT Image 2.x family holds four of the top six places. Microsoft's MAI-Image-2.7 is the best non-OpenAI model, at 1215. Google's Nano Banana 2.1 is eighth at 1175, FLUX 3 21st at 1114, and the best open-weights model, Qwen-Image-3.0, 25th at 1075 ([AA text-to-image](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). In image editing, MAI-Image-2.7 and GPT Image 2.6 are statistically tied ([AA editing](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/editing)). **Suno v6 leads both music arenas** (1148 instrumental, 1145 vocals), and ElevenLabs' music models sit roughly 125–150 Elo lower ([AA instrumental](https://artificialanalysis.ai/music/leaderboard/instrumental); [AA vocals](https://artificialanalysis.ai/music/leaderboard/vocals)). Udio is not ranked, and there is no arena for sound effects at all.

| Category | Blind-arena leader | Most used in AI jams | Most named on Steam | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2D image | GPT Image 2.5/2.6 (AA, 1263) | GPT Image (Slapjam 20/97) | Gemini/Nano Banana 8, OpenAI 6 | **Yes**: both are top-10 arena models |
| Studio concept art | Midjourney not in AA top 25 | 0 uses | 2 | **No**: GDC's top tool is unranked |
| 3D | HF arena: PicGen3D, TRELLIS, Meshy-6; Crafiq: Tripo H3.1 (≈38 votes) | Tripo (sponsored) | Meshy 7, Tripo 4 | **Unsettled**: no adequate arena covers commercial Tripo, Rodin or Hunyuan3D 3.x |
| Pixel sprites | No image-model benchmark; PixelBench (agents drawing in code): GPT-6 Astra, then Claude Opus 5 | PixelLab (rare) | PixelLab 7 | **Untested** |
| Music | Suno v6 | Suno | Suno 25 | **Yes** |
| SFX/voice | No SFX arena | ElevenLabs | ElevenLabs 12 | **Untested** |

Sources: [HF 3D Arena](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3d-arena/3d-arena), [Crafiq 3D rankings](https://crafiq.ai/rankings/3d), [PixelBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20628).

Two caveats limit how far this agreement goes. First, **the arenas measure general aesthetic preference on generic prompts, not what a game needs.** No independent benchmark ranks image models on transparent backgrounds, sprite-sheet consistency or seamless tiles. A 2026 survey of 3D generation concludes "a persistent gap separates the outputs of current methods from the production-ready standard" on topology, UVs and rigging ([arXiv 2604.23629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23629)). Second, **top arena quality has not translated into placings.** GPT Image is both the best-rated and the most-used 2D model, yet its share among Slapjam's top 10 is the same as below them. The arena tells you which model makes the nicer single picture. The jams show that a whole game is judged on coherence, and code-drawn art provides coherence by construction. PixelBench strengthens this point from the other side. Frontier coding models drawing palette-constrained pixel art through code tools were ranked by ten raters with professional art backgrounds, and nine of the ten put GPT-6 Astra first ([PixelBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20628)). The code-drawn route that Slapjam's winners used is now a measured capability, not only a workaround.

## Commercial hits disclose narrowly and seldom name any model

No study links a specific model to sales or review scores, and the data rules one out for now. Among the **12 AI-disclosing Steam games estimated at eight-figure gross, only Infection Free Zone names a model** ("Some artworks have been made with the help of the Midjourney AI tool") ([Totally Human spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ZHfowAfdR84daeoSZlvXQK5Nm7zM2Kpevf5klURViNE/edit?gid=0#gid=0)). Among 360 current top-seller and popular-new games, 35 disclose AI and **none names an image or 3D model**. The only named tools are ElevenLabs and Suno, in One More Lawn and Train Sim World 7 ([Steam top sellers](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?filter=topsellers&category1=998)). Large publishers such as Activision, Embark, Paradox and EA describe the use without naming a tool.

What the commercial data does show is a penalty for disclosure itself, and that it shrinks as the use narrows. Of 45 disclosing games estimated at $1M or more, 49% cite in-game art, 27% voice and 13% concept work ([Totally Human](https://www.totallyhuman.io/blog/games-with-ai-disclosures-have-grossed-an-estimated-660m-on-steam)). **Disclosing games average 76.4% positive reviews against 81.5% for the rest.** They made up 42% of Q3 2026 launches but only about 12% of sales. Successful games are 3.5 times as likely to drop the label (2.5% of disclosing games with 100 or more reviews against 0.7% with fewer than 10) ([Haro](https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/half-of-new-steam-games-now-disclose)). A Game Oracle analysis of comparable games found about **52.6% fewer reviews** for those with a disclosure ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-ai-disclosure-what-developers-wrote)). A study of 508,192 reviews found AI-disclosing games recommended 68.4% of the time against **86.3% for procedural-generation games** ([arXiv 2608.11539](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11539v1)). That last comparison matches the jam result: players and judges treat the procedural route differently from generated imagery. All of these are correlations. AI-disclosing games are also cheaper, in more crowded genres and more often from first-time developers, so the stigma and the quality gap cannot be separated. Industry sentiment adds pressure: 55% of GDC respondents now view generative AI negatively ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/state-of-the-game-industry-november-2026/)).

## For bomb-it-5, the art gate matters more than the model

**The faithful replica needs no art model.** Its art comes from the original SWF through the reference player, and the project rules already fix how it is used. Positions, scales and states come from SWF data, and art counts as a placeholder until the owner confirms it ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md); [docs/development.md](../docs/development.md)). Nothing in this evidence argues for replacing that. Generated art would add both the quality-coherence risk that jams penalise and the disclosure penalty that stores show, and it would bring no gain in fidelity.

**For the separate Douyin build with new art, the evidence supports three choices in priority order.** The first is to fix one style and one source before choosing a model. The pattern among placed jam entries is either agent-drawn vector or code art, or one image model used for flat UI, icons and textures with structure built by script. A maze-bomber's tiles, blocks, bombs and flames are simple geometric shapes, which suits the code-drawn route, now backed by PixelBench. That route also keeps package size small. `docs/douyin.md` records an unverified main-package budget of about 4 MB, which must be checked against official documentation before export work. The second choice, where raster art is wanted, is to make GPT Image 2.x the default. It leads the blind arenas and is the only image model shipped in placed AI-jam entries, but transparency, sprite consistency and tiling must be verified per asset, because no benchmark covers them. PixelLab is the tool most often named for pixel sprites on Steam, but it is unbenchmarked. For audio, use Suno v6 for music, which leads its arena and is the most consistent winner signal, and ElevenLabs for voice and effects, where no arena exists. The third choice is to leave 3D generators out, since the game is 2D, and to keep Midjourney out of the pipeline, because it has neither an API-ranked score nor any presence among jam entrants.

The owner remains the art gate either way. Placed entries show that a human did the art direction. Shrinestone's art log records a model silently shifting textures off their UV layouts. Judges and players punish clashing styles. A comparison loop can say that a Douyin screen is ready for review, not that it is good. One gap these notes cannot close is the Chinese platform's rules for labelling AI-generated content. Before the Douyin build ships generated art, those rules need the same verification against official sources that `docs/douyin.md` already asks for.

## Conclusion

The question "which model wins more" assumes the model is the variable that separates winners from the field, and the pooled data says it is not. Model usage is shaped by bundling (GPT Image arrives with Codex and ChatGPT) and by sponsorship (Tripo's credits). Model quality, as arenas measure it, is real but tests the wrong thing. It rates single pictures, while games are judged on whether every asset belongs to one world. That is why the two clearest winner signals, finished AI audio in Vibe Jam and code-drawn art in Slapjam, are signals of completeness and coherence rather than of any brand. The industry data on hits agrees for its own reasons: successful studios use AI narrowly and quietly, and a disclosed AI look costs reviews.

The useful change in perspective is that, for small 2D games, "which image model" is now a smaller decision than "generated pictures or code-drawn art". The second option has moved from a jam workaround to a benchmarked strength of the same coding agents developers already use. Firm model-level answers will need data that does not yet exist: Epic MegaJam results in January 2027, crawls of jams with mandatory disclosure, or Haro's non-public 65,000-game disclosure set. Until then, choose by pipeline fit, keep one style, and let a human approve the look.
